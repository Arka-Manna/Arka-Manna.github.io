21 / RC VEHICLES

RC BUGGY

A custom-built radio-controlled buggy developed as a hands-on platform for exploring mechanical design, drivetrain integration, electronics and remote vehicle control.

COMPLETED
RC VEHICLE MECHANICAL DESIGN MOTOR CONTROL RADIO CONTROL PROTOTYPING
01 / OVERVIEW

Built to move. Designed to learn.

The RC Buggy is a custom-built ground vehicle created to explore the complete development process of a small radio-controlled machine.

The project combines mechanical construction, drivetrain integration, electronics and RC control into a single working platform.

The focus was not simply getting the vehicle moving, but understanding how the mechanical and electronic systems work together to produce a responsive RC vehicle.

02 / DESIGN

A compact platform built around the drivetrain.

Mechanical System

  • Custom vehicle chassis.
  • Wheel-based ground mobility.
  • Motor and drivetrain integration.
  • Compact component arrangement.

Control System

  • Radio-controlled operation.
  • Throttle control.
  • Steering control.
  • Electronic speed control.
03 / SYSTEM

From transmitter to wheels.

The vehicle uses a conventional RC architecture in which control commands are transmitted from the pilot's transmitter to the receiver and then converted into motor and steering actions.

05 / MECHANICAL DESIGN

Engineering the machine, not just assembling it.

Building the buggy involved working with the physical constraints of a compact vehicle: component placement, chassis structure, wheel positioning and drivetrain integration.

The project provided practical experience in turning a concept into a functional mechanical prototype.

06 / TESTING

Build. Test. Improve.

Testing focused on validating the vehicle's movement, control response, steering behaviour and overall mechanical operation.

TESTED SYSTEM

The completed prototype successfully demonstrates the integration of the mechanical platform, propulsion system and RC control.

07 / RESULT

A working RC platform from the ground up.

The RC Buggy became a practical exercise in vehicle engineering, combining mechanical construction and electronics into a functional remotely controlled machine.

It also provided a foundation for experimenting with more advanced RC vehicles and robotic platforms.
