19 / FPV SYSTEMS

HEAD TRACKING

An FPV human-machine interface that translates the pilot's head movement into camera orientation, creating a more immersive first-person flying experience.

IN DEVELOPMENT
FPV IMU HEAD TRACKING WIRELESS SERVO / GIMBAL
01 / OVERVIEW

Look around. The aircraft follows.

The FPV Head Tracking System is being developed to create a natural interface between the pilot and an FPV camera.

Instead of manually controlling the camera orientation, the system detects the pilot's head movement and translates it into camera movement.

The goal is a more intuitive and immersive FPV experience where looking in a direction becomes the camera command.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Turning human movement into machine control.

Orientation Tracking

  • Detect head orientation.
  • Track yaw movement.
  • Track pitch movement.
  • Establish a stable reference position.

FPV Camera Control

  • Convert orientation into control commands.
  • Drive camera/gimbal movement.
  • Maintain smooth camera response.
  • Reduce unnecessary movement and jitter.
03 / SYSTEM

From head movement to camera movement.

The system is divided into a wearable tracking unit and a camera-side control system.

04 / CONTROL AXES

Your head becomes the camera control.

Yaw

  • Left/right head rotation.
  • Controls horizontal camera movement.
  • Enables looking around the FPV scene.

Pitch

  • Up/down head movement.
  • Controls vertical camera movement.
  • Enables looking upward or downward.

The hardware behind the interface.

06 / CONTROL LOGIC

Raw motion isn't enough.

The important part of a head-tracking system is not simply measuring movement. The orientation data must be converted into a smooth and usable control signal.

The control layer can apply calibration, dead-zone handling, scaling and smoothing before sending the final commands to the camera mechanism.

CALIBRATION FILTERING DEAD ZONE SCALING SERVO CONTROL
07 / FPV EXPERIENCE

Making FPV feel more natural.

Traditional FPV systems require the pilot to control the aircraft while independently managing camera orientation.

Head tracking creates a more natural interaction: the pilot can look toward a subject while the camera follows the same movement.

08 / DEVELOPMENT

Still being refined.

The system is currently under development, with the focus on improving tracking stability, response smoothness and reliable wireless communication.

CURRENT DEVELOPMENT

Refining orientation tracking, calibration, signal processing and camera-control response for a smooth FPV experience.

09 / DIRECTION

The goal: disappear into the flight.

The long-term objective is to create a reliable head-tracking interface that can be integrated into an FPV aircraft system without distracting the pilot from flying.

The project sits at the intersection of embedded systems, human-machine interfaces, wireless communication and RC aviation.
